The hired hands

Same consultants. Both sides of the ballot.

Every campaign and PAC has to report who it pays. Follow the business-backed money through its PACs and out the other end, and it lands with a short list of firms. Several of them also work for Portland’s best-known Democratic and progressive campaigns.

On the left: the business-backed candidates, Sharon Meieran and the PACs behind them. In the middle: the firms they paid since January 2025, sized by how much. On the right: the Democratic and progressive clients those same firms worked for this cycle. Tap a circle to follow its lines; tap a firm to open its profile.

Doug Moore, executive director of the Chamber-aligned United for Portland Action Fund, told Willamette Week the Chamber’s aim is to “stop DSA from taking over the council.” Before that came People for Portland’s ads about a “broken city” and the push to move climate money to police. These seven firms were paid by the Chamber’s PAC network, by Community Safety Coalition, or by committees that hire Kevin Looper’s firm, and also by business-backed candidates whose donors overlap with Republican and Portland conservative donors.

Case files

Two firms, in their own filings

Springwater Partners

Paige Richardson’s consulting firm. She sits on the board of Emerge Oregon, which trains Democratic women to run.

  1. 2020Paid $66K by the police-oversight measure and $168K by Chloe Eudaly.
  2. 2022Paid $418K by Jo Ann Hardesty’s campaign.
  3. Apr 2025Helped design a poll on repealing Preschool for All, pitched to Tim Boyle and other business leaders. She told Willamette Week, “I often don’t agree with them on policy issues.”
  4. Aug 2026Paid $10K by Community Safety Coalition, the PAC behind the failed initiative to move Clean Energy Fund money to police. Its big funders: the police union, Jeff Swickard ($250K) and Tim Boyle ($100K).
  5. Oct 2026Paid $103K by Olivia Clark and $99K by Kellie Torres between Oct 7 and Oct 14.

Swickard’s company also gave the Chamber’s PAC $150K this cycle, and Swickard and Boyle each gave Go Portland Go $25K.

Untidy Origins

Portland ad and video shop. Clients this cycle include OEA, the Working Families Party, the Portland teachers’ PAC and Tobias Read.

  1. Oct 1Paid $44K by Steve Novick.
  2. Oct 5Paid $49K by Julia Brim-Edwards, with $34K more owed.
  3. Oct 6Paid $30K by Mitch Green, the progressive councilor the Chamber’s billboards attack.
  4. Oct 8Paid another $36K by Novick.
