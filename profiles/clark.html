Olivia Clark

incumbent

Clark sided with the business bloc on 74 of 77 split votes and led the push to move $10M from voter-created police accountability.

Who they are

Clark is the council's vice president, a Democrat, and one of its most moderate members (OPB, Oct 7, 2026). Before the council she was Gov. Kitzhaber's liaison to cities and counties, then spent more than 20 years at TriMet, where she was executive director of public affairs and built the coalition that funded light rail and Tilikum Crossing (WWeek, Aug 28, 2024; portland.gov bio). She calls herself "a seasoned, pragmatic professional, an optimist, and a collaborator."

The problem

Olivia Clark is an experienced public servant and WWeek's top pick in District 4. On the questions that divide Portland's council, though, she is one of the business bloc's most dependable votes. When the council split along bloc lines in 2025–26, she sided with the centrists on 74 of the 77 votes she cast, including every split on police, homelessness, labor and climate [1].

Police accountability is the clearest case. In 2020, 82% of voters created a new independent oversight system. In May 2026, Clark was lead sponsor of an amendment to move $10 million out of the new Office of Community Police Accountability to restore police and fire cuts. It failed 6–6 [2]. WWeek reports she was the first councilor to suggest tapping the office. The July supplemental budget she co-sponsored would again have refilled reserves from it, until an amendment removed that directive. One board member called that plan "blatant interference" with the office's independence [3]. About a week after the May vote, she voted against requiring all officers, federal agents included, to show their faces and ID [4]. In 2025 she opposed shifting $1.9 million in new police money to parks, which she reportedly called the "absolutely wrong signal" [5].

On city services and workers, she voted no on the plan to restore up to 55 frontline jobs and backed a rival plan that saved 30 and added police training money [6]. In the May budget she voted against backfilling 46 non-manager positions, against capping non-union raises at the first $100,000 of salary, and against restoring Office of Equity staff, including the Human Rights Commission officer [7].

On climate and homelessness, she voted to let more than $5 million in clean-energy fund money move to encampment-removal programs, and against funding democratically governed villages [7]. At the City Club debate she said she is "very interested in looking at sending a measure to the voters" to shift clean-energy money to police, fire and parks [8]. She voted to transfer the Zenith oil terminal franchise, which passed only on the mayor's tie-break [9]. She also voted against an amendment the Mercury describes as letting citizens enforce that agreement [10].

On public money and openness, she voted for $120 million up front plus up to $275 million in maintenance for the Blazers' arena. She also voted against a Morillo/Kanal amendment to the term sheet, which failed 6–6 [11]. She calls it "a great deal" [8]. She voted against requiring the city administrator to release records on unspent housing money and hold an oversight hearing, and backed an audit alternative instead [12]. She answered "Easy, oppose" on the participatory budgeting charter measure and says she looks forward to changing the voter-approved charter [8].

Her backers fit the record. The Portland Metro Chamber's PAC tells D4 voters to rank Zimmerman and Clark and no one else [13]. Unity PAC, which includes the Chamber's PAC and the police union, recommends only those two [14].

Clark has real accomplishments: the street fee, an asset-management plan, tighter earthquake rules for the fuel hub, and co-sponsoring a framework to respond to federal immigration raids. But on police oversight, city jobs, climate money and transparency, hers has repeatedly been one of the six votes that stopped progressive proposals.

Key votes and actions

2026-05-19

Led the push to take $10M from police accountability

Clark was lead sponsor of an amendment moving $10M from the Office of Community Police Accountability, which 82% of voters created in 2020, to partly restore police and fire cuts. It failed 6–6. WWeek says she was the first councilor to suggest tapping the office.

Willamette Week
2026-06-25 to 2026-07-22

Co-sponsored a July plan to refill reserves from the accountability office

As introduced, the July supplemental budget she co-sponsored would refill the city's contingency mainly with Office of Community Police Accountability money. An amendment removed that directive before it passed 10–2 (Ord. 192207).

Willamette Week
2025-01 to 2026-10

Sided with the business bloc on 96% of split votes

When the council split along bloc lines in 2025–26, Clark sided with the centrists on 74 of 77 votes she cast. She went 16–0 on police and public safety splits and 9–0 on Zenith and climate splits. This tally is our analysis of portland.gov roll calls and Portland Mercury live blogs.

2026-09-09 and 2026-09-23

Helped keep the Zenith oil terminal franchise alive

Clark voted against the Green-Morillo amendment to the transfer ordinance (failed 6–6). She then voted yes on the franchise transfer, which passed only on Mayor Wilson's tie-break.

portland.gov
2026-07-22

Voted no on restoring 55 city jobs; backed a smaller plan

Clark voted no on 2026-222, which used PCEF interest and other funds to restore up to 55 frontline positions. It failed when the mayor broke a 6–6 tie. She co-sponsored the rival plan, which saved 30 jobs and added $3M for police training.

portland.gov
2026-05-20

Climate money to encampment programs; no on resident-run villages

Clark voted no on reversing the mayor's shift of more than $5M in PCEF climate funds to PEMO and the Impact Reduction Program (encampment cleanup and removal programs), which failed 6–6. She also voted no on Green's amendment funding democratically governed villages, which failed 6–6.

Portland Mercury
2026-08-12

Yes on $120M for the Blazers' arena; no on a Morillo/Kanal amendment

Clark voted yes on the Moda Center term sheet: $120M up front plus up to $275M in maintenance over 20 years. It passed 8–4. She voted no on a Morillo/Kanal amendment, which failed 6–6.

portland.gov
2026-02-04

Voted no on releasing records on unspent housing money

Clark voted no on Green's resolution requiring the City Administrator to release records on unspent housing funds and schedule an oversight hearing (Res. 37734, adopted 8–4). She backed a failed alternative directing an investigation or audit instead. The city later identified an estimated $106M in unbudgeted housing funds (KPTV).

portland.gov
Oct 2026

Wants voters asked to move climate money to police, fire, parks

At the City Club debate, Clark said she is "very interested in looking at sending a measure to the voters" to shift part of the Portland Clean Energy Fund to police, fire and parks.

Clark voted no on the 2025 Avalos amendment shifting $1.9M the mayor earmarked for police staffing to parks maintenance (passed 7–5). WWeek reports she said it sent the "absolutely wrong signal."

What they'd say

Clark says the $10M would have restored deep police and fire cuts while the accountability office had no director, and the amendment promised repayment (WWeek, May 20, 2026). The city attorney said the mask/ID ordinance's federal-officer provisions are likely unconstitutional (WWeek, May 27, 2026), and Zenith had sued the city over the delay (KPTV). She also led the street fee, which she says reuses the water bureau's low-income discount (Mercury; City Club debate), and the asset-management plan. She co-sponsored tighter earthquake-safety rules for the fuel hub (KPTV, Oct 3, 2026) and the Protect Portland Initiative on immigration raids (her newsletter). She voted yes on the 2025 Zenith investigation (passed 11–1), the ICE facility fee and senior parks programming. WWeek ranks her first in D4 for her "straightforward manner, lack of drama," and the Street Trust Action Fund endorses her (Mercury).
