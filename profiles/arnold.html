Eli Arnold

challenger

A sitting bike cop who co-founded the group bankrolling Go Portland Go and in 2024 proposed using clean-energy money for police hiring.

Who they are

Arnold is a Portland police officer on the Central Bike Squad, an Army veteran and former Blackhawk pilot who joined the bureau in 2017, and placed fourth in District 4 in 2024 (WWeek, Jul 31, 2024; WWeek, Nov 8, 2025). The Mercury reports he was on the bureau's riot response squad in 2020 and helped found Partnership for Progress in early 2025 (Sept 11, 2026). His 2026 voters' pamphlet statement calls him a "results focused, practical progressive" and lists him as a 2016 Bernie Sanders delegate and Multnomah County Democrats PCP. OPB says his downtown police work focused on "cracking down on high-volume fentanyl dealers" (Oct 7, 2026).

The problem

Eli Arnold has never held office, so the case rests on what he has said and whom he has organized with. Both point the same way: more police money, no new revenue, and pressure on Portland's climate fund.

Start with the climate fund. In 2024, asked how he would pay to hire officers toward a goal of 1.8 per 1,000 residents, Arnold said "We should use some of the PCEF revenue which greatly exceeded the program size sold to voters," and called for moving PCEF revenue above projections to the general fund [1]. In early 2025 he co-founded Partnership for Progress with Vadim Mozyrsky and Bob Weinstein [2][3]. The Mercury reports the group backed the failed Portland Enhanced Community Safety initiative [2], which would have taken 25% of Clean Energy Fund revenue, about $50 million a year, to hire 400 officers [4]. At the October City Club debate he said it is "perfectly appropriate to ask voters" whether to reallocate PCEF "to core services," and, asked where the city's Moda Center share would come from, said "the only place it's been identified is [PCEF]" [5].

Next, the police budget. In May 2025, as the council debated moving $1.9 million from police hiring to parks maintenance, Arnold spoke at a rally against the transfer [6]. Partnership for Progress, which he co-led, warned the council's budget vote would decide whether Portland would "spiral further into decline" [3]. The amendment passed 7-5 [7]. The same group, per the Mercury, has advocated for homeless camp sweeps and opposed the Preschool for All tax on high earners [2]. Arnold himself has said Preschool for All "would have been much better as a voucher program" [1].

Then, revenue. His 2026 pamphlet says "I will not support raising taxes and fees" [8]. In 2024 he said "I oppose any increase in total tax burden on Portlanders" [1]. In 2026 he framed the city's budget gap as a possible "spending problem," comparing Portland's budget to Boston's and Denver's [9].

On homelessness, he called ending street camping his top priority in 2024, proposing designated camping locations with people registered by name [10]. Asked about jail, he said "Jail is not our goal," but that for those who refuse basic rules, "we need to apply pressure" [1].

An analysis by the police-accountability group Ctrl-Alt-Defund ranks him first of 465 officers with reported force in 2021, with 18 uses, 16 involving people listed as transient, and tied for fifth of 364 in the partial 2023 data, with 10 [11]. The analysis does not show any use was out of policy, but it is a fair question for a candidate running on enforcement.

Finally, money. Go Portland Go, which endorses Arnold, is mainly funded by the group he co-founded, with Columbia Sportswear CEO Tim Boyle and auto-dealer Jeff Swickard as its next-largest donors [2]. Boyle also gave $100,000 to the clean-energy-for-police effort [2]. Willamette Week, no progressive outlet, ranked Arnold last in District 4: "his backing by Portland's wealthy class makes us worry he'd answer to them, not the average Portlander" [12].

Arnold calls himself a practical progressive, and he does support fareless transit and shelter as a moral duty [8][9]. He also opposes this year's participatory budgeting measure [5]. But on the fights that define this council, police money, climate dollars and revenue, his record points away from progressive priorities.

Key votes and actions

2021 and 2023 data

Police-accountability group's analysis ranks him #1 of 465 officers in reported force (2021)

According to an analysis by the police-accountability group Ctrl-Alt-Defund of Portland Police Bureau force data, Arnold had 18 uses of force in 2021, ranking #1 of 465 officers with reported force; it lists the count as 409% above average, with 16 involving subjects listed as transient. In 2023 (a partial year in the data) it lists him tied for 5th of 364 officers, with 10 uses. The analysis does not show that any use was found out of policy.

What they'd say

Arnold runs as a Sanders-delegate progressive who wants police alongside alternatives ("I think we need both"), cites his service on the Police Review Board, and calls adequate shelter a moral obligation (BikePortland, Apr 2024; OPB, Sept 2024; NW Examiner, Mar 2026). He backs using clean-energy money toward fareless transit, and his endorsers include Moms Demand Action, Amanda Fritz and Mike Lindberg (pamphlet; Mercury, Sept 2026). At the City Club debate he said he does not fit "neatly" into categories, citing his Sanders delegacy, and backed connecting shelters to jobs and treatment. The Chamber's PAC and Unity PAC both decline to rank him, and WWeek calls him "one of the good apples, we hear." A downtown bike-squad officer who makes many proactive drug arrests may log more force reports than average, and an analysis by the police-accountability group Ctrl-Alt-Defund does not show that any use was found out of policy.
