Julia Brim-Edwards

challenger (current county commissioner, District 3; open chair seat)

A former Nike lobbyist who helped sink Metro's 2020 transit tax, voted $101.6M for the Blazers' arena, and lost Basic Rights Oregon's endorsement.

Who they are

Brim-Edwards has been Multnomah County's District 3 commissioner since 2023 and served two stints on the Portland Public Schools board. Before that she was a Nike executive and lobbyist after "a first career in Oregon politics," which included serving as Sen. Bob Packwood's communications director (1992) and working for House Speaker Bev Clarno in the 1990s. She calls herself a results-focused leader who "disrupted the status quo," and her campaign is backed by the Chamber's PAC.

The problem

Julia Brim-Edwards is the most experienced insider in the race, and that is the problem. Before county office she spent years as a Nike executive and lobbyist, "involved in nearly every significant state and local political issue" for 17 years [1]. WWeek reported that she "played a central role in defeating Metro's massive transportation tax in 2020" [1], a measure Nike spent $914,000 to beat [2]. It was a multibillion-dollar regional measure for transit and street projects [2]. In 2018 she was named in a deal brokered by Gov. Kate Brown, in which unions dropped a ballot measure that would have forced public companies to disclose their taxes, a proposal Nike disliked. Nike paid $100,000 to a PAC she founded; regulators found no violation [3].

On the county board, her signature moves lean toward enforcement. In 2024 she proposed an "overarching camping ban throughout Multnomah County" [4], and her budget letter sets a goal of "ending street camping" and asks for Good Neighbor Agreements before new shelters open [5]. In 2023 she pushed to delay a fentanyl harm-reduction supply program [6]. In the 2025 budget fight she sided with the DA, and the DA and Sheriff were the only offices spared cuts [7]. She proposed an amendment to restore more than $1 million to the DA's office, funded by trimming vacant positions [8].

On revenue, she twice proposed indexing the Preschool for All tax thresholds to inflation. Supporters said that would shrink the funding behind universal free preschool, and the board shelved it [9][10]. On the Moda Center she objected to paying "for maintenance for an asset we don't own," and had backed a smaller $88M deal, then voted for $101.6 million [11]. WWeek says she had helped craft the package "in closed-door meetings" [12].

Her record on openness worries people who worked with her. At PPS she led efforts to tone down a 2019 state audit and held a press conference attacking it before release [13]. Seven current and former school board members, including the current chair, call her leadership "ineffective" and say she chose "the most politically expedient path" [14]. WWeek, which still ranks her second, sees "a worrying pattern" of expediency over principle [12]. Basic Rights Oregon PAC withdrew its endorsement over her reported comments on trans student athletes [15].

Her money and allies point the same way. The Chamber's PAC, United for Portland, tells voters to rank her first and nobody else [16]. Unity PAC, which includes the police union and United for Portland, backs only her [17]. Her donors include Chamber executives and developer Jordan Schnitzer [18], and she has far outraised her rivals [19].

She has real labor and Democratic endorsements, and some of her fights, like questioning bad preschool forecasts, have merit. But on transit funding, corporate tax transparency, homelessness, arena subsidies and public accountability, her record lines up more often with the business groups funding her campaign than with the progressive goals voters set at the ballot box.

Key votes and actions

2020-11-03 (election); reported 2020-11-09 and 2021-11-23

Played "central role" defeating Metro's 2020 transportation tax

WWeek reported that Brim-Edwards, then a Nike executive, "played a central role in defeating Metro's massive transportation tax in 2020." Nike spent $914,000 against the measure, a little more than a third of what the "no" campaign raised.

Willamette Week (with WWeek, Nov 9, 2020 on Nike's $914K: https://www.wweek.com/news/2020/11/09/nike-spent-more-than-900000-to-beat-metros-multi-billion-transportation-tax/)
2026-10-06

BRO says she raised trans-athlete concerns; it pulled its endorsement

BRO PAC's board voted unanimously to withdraw its endorsement. It said she raised concerns in private PPS board meetings that trans athletes could hurt other students' scholarship chances, and that she urged stricter participation rules. BRO says it verified these accounts through confidential briefings, so the specifics are not public.

Basic Rights Oregon PAC (statement)
2026-08-06

Voted for $101.6 million in county money for the Moda Center

The county board approved $101.6M toward renovating the Blazers' arena 4-1, and Brim-Edwards voted yes. Only Commissioner Meghan Moyer voted no, saying the amount was "too much." Brim-Edwards had first pushed for a smaller $88M commitment paid only from the rental-car tax.

OPB
2018-12 to 2019-04-12; letter 2026-07-29

Fought a state audit of PPS, then volunteered for the new Secretary of State

As chair of the board's audit committee, she "took a leadership role in the district's efforts to tone down" the Secretary of State's 2019 PPS audit. She held a pre-release press conference criticizing it, and the SOS office called the district's early disclosure an act of "bad faith." Months later she volunteered on new Secretary of State Bev Clarno's transition and ended the role in April 2019 after it drew questions. Seven current and former school board members later wrote that she hid this role from district officials.

Oregon Capital Bureau via East Oregonian (also OPB Jan 9, 2019: https://www.opb.org/news/article/portland-public-schools-audit-secretary-of-state-release/ ; OPB Apr 12, 2019: https://www.opb.org/news/article/portland-school-board-member-ends-voluntary-work-secretary-of-state/ ; WWeek Jul 29, 2026: https://www.wweek.com/news/county/2026/07/29/seven-former-and-current-portland-school-board-members-warn-julia-brim-edwards-is-not-fit-for-county-chair/)
2026-07-29

Seven school board colleagues say she is unfit to lead

Seven current and former PPS board members, including current chair Eddie Wang, signed a letter calling her an "ineffective" leader whose micromanagement fed a "toxic" environment. They also said she took the "most politically expedient path" over what the data and student outcomes showed. Two of the signers endorse her opponent Shannon Singleton.

Willamette Week
2025-08-21

Proposed raising the income line for the Preschool for All tax

She proposed indexing the voter-approved tax's income thresholds to inflation, starting at $153,000 for single filers and $245,000 for joint filers. Supporters said that would cut revenue and put universal free preschool at risk. The board postponed the change indefinitely, and one supporter accused commissioners of "kicking the can down the road." She had offered a similar indexing amendment in 2024, and the board did not adopt it.

KATU (also OPB Aug 21, 2025: https://www.opb.org/article/2025/08/21/multnomah-county-delay-preschool-for-all-funding/ ; Multnomah County, Aug 29, 2024: https://multco.us/news/news-release-board-weighs-one-year-pause-scheduled-increase-preschool-all-tax-high-earners)
2024-09-18

Proposed a countywide homeless camping ban

In September 2024 she proposed an "overarching camping ban throughout Multnomah County" covering unincorporated areas and county property, enforced by city police or the sheriff. She told KGW "It absolutely needs to be done," and also said the focus should be more shelter, with punishment as a last resort. No adoption was found in coverage.

KGW 'The Story' video description, Sept 18, 2024 (also KGW article: https://kgw.com/article/news/local/homeless/multnomah-county-homeless-camping-ban-commissioner-brim-edwards/283-4b94308f-4e49-4329-92c9-8cc59fbecfa4 ; KPTV Sept 18, 2024: https://www.kptv.com/2024/09/19/multnomah-co-officials-consider-new-camping-ban)

2025-05-08 to 2025-06-12

Sided with the DA in the 2025 budget fight

She proposed an amendment to restore more than $1 million to the DA's office, funded by trimming six vacant positions, and co-sponsored $500,000 for an Organized Retail Theft Task Force. 2023-07-10

Pushed to delay a fentanyl harm-reduction supply program

In July 2023 she and Commissioner Sharon Meieran asked the health department to delay handing out foil and straws to fentanyl smokers. She said there was "no compelling evidence that it is comparable to safe needle exchanges."

What they'd say

Her endorsers are broad and mostly Democratic or labor: former Govs. Kulongoski and Roberts, Secretary of State Tobias Read, U.S. Reps. Bonamici, Bynum and Hoyle, all six county mayors, the Oregon Nurses Association, AFSCME Local 88 and the NW Oregon Labor Council (Mercury Oct 12; WWeek Apr 13, 2026). At Nike she worked with public employee unions to pass the 2019 Student Success Act (WWeek Nov 23, 2021), and in the 2018 deal Nike joined unions against anti-tax Measures 103 and 104 (OPB/JPR). On Moda she pushed for a smaller, tourism-tax-only deal, and WWeek says her Preschool for All forecast challenge proved right. She frames her camping and deflection positions as shelter- and treatment-first, co-sponsoring the 2026 deflection resolution with Singleton (multco.us). On PPS she says her board record "speaks for itself."
