2
How ranked choice works

Rank the people you want to win. Then stop.

  1. 1

    Your first choice comes first.

    Ranking someone 2nd or 3rd never hurts your 1st choice.

  2. 2

    Your vote keeps working.

    If your first choice wins or is knocked out, your vote moves to your next choice.

  3. 3

    Leave the rest blank.

    Anyone you rank can end up with your vote. It’s fine to rank one, two or three people.

  4. 4

    One bubble per column.

    Don’t give two people the same rank. Council districts elect three people; the Chair race elects one.

Portland City Council · District 3
Rank1or2

Tiffany Koyama Lane

Why rank herA Portland public school teacher for 16 years, Tiffany is the first Asian American woman elected to Portland City Council. When the new council first met, her colleagues unanimously elected her its first vice president.1 She led the city’s Vision Zero work to end traffic deaths,2 added family and relationship structure to Portland’s civil rights protections,3 and went through the budget line by line to save 14 tree permitting jobs.4

“the socialist councilor we most trust to reach across whatever constitutes an aisle”— Willamette Week4

What Tiffany has done

  • Vision Zero. Reconvened the city’s Vision Zero task force36 and co-led dedicating a quarter of the transportation fee to Vision Zero and sidewalks. Passed 9–3.37

How we picked

People-powered, not donor-powered.

We recommend candidates who stand up for renters, city services and the police accountability office voters created, and whose donors over $100 don’t overlap with Republican campaigns.

Our council picks run on small gifts: 95–98% of their small-donor gifts were $100 or less, through the city’s Small Donor Elections program. We checked every donor who gave over $100 against Oregon campaign finance records; smaller gifts aren’t named in those records. The method is on our Receipts page.

This guide is independent. No candidate, campaign or committee asked for it, paid for it or reviewed it.
