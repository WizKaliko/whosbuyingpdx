The doom loop

Portland isn't failing. It's recovering unevenly.

The Portland Metro Chamber gave the city its scariest phrase, and the groups funding the committees behind these candidates keep telling the same story of decline. Even Mayor Wilson pushed back, urging "boom loop thinking" WWeek.

Partnership for Progress's newsletters have featured what the Mercury calls "satirical, AI-generated images that spin narratives of Portland in decline" (Mercury).

The city's report counts 2019 as 30 and 2024 as about 68; counts differ by definition. Still above 2019. Crime fell nationwide too, so no one politician owns the drop.
Better than the doom story says

Homicides 101 in 2022 → 52 in 2025.

Car thefts down about two-thirds from the 2022 peak. PPB

Downtown foot traffic 81% of 2019 levels in August 2026, the best month since 2020, according to Placer.ai data in a Downtown Portland Clean & Safe report. Axios

Population edged up in 2025 (+0.2%), though it's still below 2020. OPB

Overdose deaths in Oregon down about 25% last year. Axios

Still hurting, and worth saying

Empty offices about a third of downtown office space is vacant (34.7%, CBRE, late 2025); the national rate is about 20% (Cushman & Wakefield). Axios · IREI

Jobs one of only two big U.S. metros to lose jobs in the year to June 2026, across the whole region, not just the city. BLS

Housing permits sharply down. OPB

Homelessness the county's one-night count rose 67% from 2023 to 2025. Better counting explains much of that, but the number likely didn't fall. KATU · WWeek

Those are real problems to solve. They aren't proof that Portland is failing, and they aren't an argument for handing the city to the people selling the doom.
