Who's buying

Much of their outside support runs through one pipe: the United for Portland Action Fund, the Chamber's PAC. It has raised $412K this cycle, almost all of it from the Chamber's political arm, a company owned by auto dealer and downtown landlord Jeff Swickard, and the Realtors. In October it sent nearly $200K to Unity PAC, a new business-and-labor committee backing moderates. In September it sent $160K to goPDX, which funds billboard committees with neighborhood-sounding names.

A complaint filed Oct 5 says the billboard setup hid where the money came from. That's an allegation; no one has ruled on it.

The donors

Their donors include Republican and conservative money.

We checked every donor who gave over $100 to ten candidates against up to 20 years of contributors to 25 Republican campaigns, caucus and party committees, and to six other Portland conservative campaigns and committees (Gonzalez, Vasquez, Mozyrsky, Adams, Treece, and a 2022 PAC backing Gonzalez). We count a local campaign as Portland conservative only if its donors overlap the business-backed candidates and not the progressive councilors running against them. A gift counts only when its own street address or PO box (same street, quadrant and city or ZIP), or ZIP code and employer, matches the donor's. Some matches were confirmed by hand from filing records instead: 7 of the 125 donor households with Republican gifts rest only on hand review. Counted candidate by candidate, 10 of Torres's 32 Republican-giving donors were confirmed by hand review.

The wild card

Steve Novick's votes haven't moved right. His new donors lean that way.

On the council record, Novick hasn't drifted right. But the people who gave to him for the first time in 2025–26 were more than twice as likely to have backed Republicans or Portland conservatives as the people who first gave in his 2023–24 campaign. We can't say why: 2025–26 is also when the Chamber's committees and Go Portland Go endorsed him.

Sources and methodVotes: bloc splits on the City Council, 2025 through Oct 15, 2026 (see The votes). Donors: donors who gave Novick over $100 since Jan 2023, grouped by their first gift to any Novick campaign since 2011, matched by the same method as above. The 14% to 34% jump is statistically significant (Fisher's exact test, p ≈ 0.001): if nothing had really changed, a gap this large would turn up by chance about once in 1,000 tries. Counting Republican donors alone, it's 3% to 10% (p ≈ 0.05), a borderline rise that could still be chance. Donors who first backed him before 2023, in his earlier career, were 29%. For comparison, 35% of Clark's donors over $100 and 49% of Zimmerman's have given to Republicans or Portland conservatives; among the progressive councilors running against them, none have.
