How ranked choice works

Rank the people you want to win. Then stop.

  1. 1

    Your first choice comes first.

    Ranking someone 2nd or 3rd never hurts your 1st choice.

  2. 2

    Your vote keeps working.

    If your first choice wins or is knocked out, your vote moves to your next choice.

  3. 3

    Leave the rest blank.

    Anyone you rank can end up with your vote. It’s fine to rank one, two or three people.

  4. 4

    One bubble per column.

    Don’t give two people the same rank. Council districts elect three people; the Chair race elects one.

Tiffany Koyama Lane

Why rank herA Portland public school teacher for 16 years, Tiffany was elected the council’s vice president by a unanimous vote of her colleagues.1 She led the city’s Vision Zero work to end traffic deaths,2 wrote protections for every kind of family into Portland’s civil rights code,3 and went through the budget line by line to save 14 tree-care jobs.1

What Tiffany has done

  • Vision Zero. Led the city’s work to end traffic deaths, and co-sponsored dedicating 12.5% of the transportation utility fee to it.2
  • Every family. Added family and relationship structure to Portland’s civil rights protections. Passed 11–0.3
  • Trees and parks. Restored $3.2 million to keep 14 tree permitting staff in the 2026 budget.1
  • Police accountability. Voted to keep $10 million in the oversight office voters created.12
  • Steady hands. Helped guide the council when the Trump administration tried to send National Guard troops to Portland.1
  • Safe streets for everyone
  • Every family protected
  • Parks and urban trees
  • A teacher’s eye on the budget

How we picked

People-powered, not donor-powered.

We recommend candidates who stand up for renters, city services and the police accountability office voters created, and whose donors don’t overlap with Republican campaigns.

Our council picks run on small gifts: 95–98% of their small-donor gifts were $100 or less, through the city’s Small Donor Elections program. We checked every named donor against Oregon campaign finance records. The method is on our Receipts page.

This guide is independent. No candidate, campaign or committee asked for it, paid for it or reviewed it.
