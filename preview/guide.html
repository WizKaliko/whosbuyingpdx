Who we’d rank.

Our picks for Portland City Council in Districts 3 and 4 and for Multnomah County Chair. Each one stands up for renters, city services and police accountability, and almost none of their named donors also fund Republicans.

How ranking works

Rank only people you’d want to win.

  • Your first choice comes first. Ranking someone second never hurts your first choice.
  • Anyone you rank can end up with your vote. If your first choice wins or is eliminated, your vote can move to your next choice.
  • You don’t have to fill every column. It’s fine to rank one or two people and stop.
  • One bubble per column. Don’t give two candidates the same rank, or one candidate two ranks.

Council districts elect three people each. A candidate wins with more than 25% of the vote after rankings are counted. County Chair elects one person, by majority.

Portland City Council · District 33 seats · 21 candidates

Rank Angelita Morillo 1st, Tiffany Koyama Lane 2nd and Esther León 3rd.

Angelita Morillo

Renter, immigrant and former policy advocate at Partners for a Hunger-Free Oregon. Worked three years in Commissioner Jo Ann Hardesty’s office.1

  • Wrote Portland’s ban on rent-setting software that lets landlords coordinate prices. Passed 8–2, Nov 2025.2
  • With Mitch Green, passed an impact fee on landlords who lease to immigration detention facilities. 9–2, Dec 2025.3
  • Voted no on moving $10M out of the police accountability office voters created. Failed 6–6, May 2026.4
  • Led the plan to restore 55 frontline city jobs. Tied 6–6; the mayor broke the tie against it, July 2026.5

2,398 small-donor gifts, 95% of them $100 or less.6 0 of 203 named donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives.

Endorsed by the Portland Mercury (#1 in District 3),7 Sierra Club, SEIU Oregon, Working Families Party, Basic Rights Oregon PAC and Portland DSA.

What critics say

Willamette Week ranks her fifth and calls her style divisive.8 Even the Mercury, which ranks her first, says she could pick her battles more carefully.7 Several of her budget proposals failed.

Tiffany Koyama Lane

Former Sunnyside Environmental School teacher and Portland Association of Teachers organizer. The council’s first-ever vice president.9

  • Led Vision Zero work on traffic deaths, and co-sponsored dedicating 12.5% of the transportation utility fee to it, spring 2026.10
  • Updated the city’s civil rights code to protect family structure, including nontraditional families. Passed 11–0, Mar 2026.11
  • Voted no on the $10M police-oversight cut, and restored 14 urban forestry jobs in the May 2026 budget.12
  • Voted no on the Blazers arena deal; says the city shouldn’t pay unless the team’s owners do.13

“every traffic death is preventable”15

2,509 small-donor gifts, 95% of them $100 or less.6 0 of 269 named donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives.

Endorsed by the Portland Mercury (#2),7 Willamette Week (#2),8 Sierra Club, Portland Association of Teachers, SEIU Oregon and Basic Rights Oregon.

What critics say

A former aide filed complaints in February 2026 alleging retaliation; she declined to comment, citing personnel rules.14 The Mercury reports the city investigated and dismissed it.7 Several of her priorities failed on 6–6 votes.

Esther León

Doctor of physical therapy who works with people in Oregon prisons, and a community college teacher. Has lived in District 3 since 2020. First run for office.32

  • Wants Portland Street Response running 24/7 citywide.33
  • Backs social housing and a vacancy tax on corporate landlords, and prefers supportive housing to overnight-only shelters.33
  • Would revoke the ICE facility’s permit and pause new AI data centers.34
  • On the Blazers arena: no clean-energy fund money, and a guaranteed return for the public.34

“life doesn’t have to be this way”36

383 small-donor gifts, 98% of them $100 or less.6 0 of 6 named donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives.

Endorsed by Sierra Club20 and Portland for All (#3 in District 3).35 The Mercury gives her an honorable mention.7

What critics say

She has no political or civic experience. The Mercury called one idea, bringing back public payphones, “admirable but perhaps lofty.”36 Portland Civic Lab notes her housing and utility plans don’t list costs.34

Portland City Council · District 43 seats · 12 candidates

Rank Mitch Green 1st, Jamey Evenstar 2nd and Jeremy Beausoleil Smith 3rd.

Mitch Green

Economist and Army veteran. Spent over a decade as an energy-economics analyst at the Bonneville Power Administration and taught at PSU.16

  • Co-sponsored “Keep Portland Housed”: $17.5M to start a social housing loan fund and about $20.5M for eviction prevention, April 2026.17
  • Co-wrote the rent-setting software ban and the detention-facility impact fee with Morillo.18
  • Voted no on the $10M police-oversight cut. Failed 6–6.4
  • Pushed “Save Our Services” to keep 55 city jobs. It tied 6–6 and the mayor broke the tie against it; a smaller package saving about 30 jobs then passed.19

“permanently affordable housing built for people, not portfolios”24

2,533 small-donor gifts, 95% of them $100 or less.6 0 of 227 named donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives.

Endorsed by the Portland Mercury (#1 in District 4),18 Sierra Club,20 SEIU Oregon, AFSCME, Portland Association of Teachers and Basic Rights Oregon PAC.

What critics say

He paid a $7,328 property tax installment late, after a reporter asked about it.18 He burned a brush pile during a burn ban in July and called it “a profoundly stupid thing to do.”21 The police union called for him to resign over 2020–21 tweets; he declined.22 Billboards attacking him are funded through a Chamber-aligned committee; a complaint says the funding was hidden, which the Secretary of State is reviewing.23

Jamey Evenstar

Ten years on City Hall staff, most recently chief of staff to Councilor Candace Avalos. Mother of five who rides transit every day.18

  • As policy director for Commissioner Chloe Eudaly, worked on tenant protections and the Rose Lane bus projects.37
  • Served on the team that set up Portland’s new form of government.18
  • Wants a city housing fund and a housing impact fee on homes that aren’t anyone’s primary residence.38
  • Called the original Blazers arena deal “a terrible deal.”38

“I believe Portland can do hard things.”37

662 small-donor gifts, 95% of them $100 or less.6 0 of 35 named donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives.

Endorsed by the Portland Mercury (#2 in District 4),18 Portland for All,39 Sierra Club,20 Street Trust Action Fund40 and Building Power for Communities of Color.41

What critics say

Critics point to her years working for Eudaly and Avalos. A text from her Eudaly years, released through a records request, said neighborhood associations “get too much power and voice.”42 Civic Lab found no stated position on hiring more officers or funding camp removals.38

Jeremy Beausoleil Smith

Construction manager for capital projects at Portland State University and former project engineer. Democratic Party precinct committee person.43

  • Proposes a renters’ bill of rights: no rent increases while code violations go unfixed, six months’ notice, and a right to a lawyer in eviction.44
  • Wants Portland Street Response running 24/7 citywide, and an auditor’s review of police overtime before more police money.45
  • Would pause new AI data centers and stop expansion of the riverside fuel-tank farm.44
  • Organized against a PGE project in Forest Park.37

“I’ll fight to lower costs and make life easier.”43

1,115 small-donor gifts, 98% of them $100 or less.6 1 of 17 named donors also gave to Portland conservatives (Vasquez, Adams); none to Republicans.

Endorsed by Portland for All (#3 in District 4)39 and PSU’s faculty union.45 The Mercury gives him an honorable mention.18

What critics say

The Mercury says he’d face a learning curve.18 He dropped out of the 2024 council race.37 Civic Lab notes his plans don’t name a funding source.44

Multnomah County Chair1 seat · 3 candidates

Rank Shannon Singleton first.

Shannon Singleton

Social worker and sitting county commissioner (District 2). Led the nonprofit JOIN, was interim director of the Joint Office of Homeless Services, and was Gov. Kate Brown’s housing advisor. Won her seat in 2024 over Sam Adams.25

  • Won a county eviction-prevention and legal-aid package in the 2025 budget, including eviction defense lawyers and courthouse legal help.26
  • Added money for lobbying transparency and fair housing investigations in East County (same budget).26
  • Got the stalled Walnut Park redevelopment in Albina moving.27
  • Convened the county’s Eviction Prevention Table, Feb 2026.28

“I want to serve where I can make the most change.”25

2 of 172 of Singleton’s individual donors also gave to Republicans or Portland conservatives. Brim-Edwards: 202 of 489.

Endorsed by the Portland Mercury,27 Portland for All, Portland Association of Teachers, Working Families Party, Sierra Club and Basic Rights Oregon PAC.29

What critics say

She has raised far less money than Brim-Edwards.30 She led the Joint Office of Homeless Services in 2022, and opponents may tie her to its record. SEIU Oregon endorsed both, ranking Brim-Edwards first.31

Before you rank anyone else

Julia Brim-Edwards41%Voted for $101.6M in county money for the Blazers arena. 76 of her donors also gave to Republican campaigns. Basic Rights Oregon PAC withdrew its endorsement of her on Oct 6.46 Profile →

Sharon Meieran is also running. We don’t make a recommendation on her. If you rank anyone after Singleton, rank only someone you’d want to win.

How we picked

The record and the receipts.

We recommend candidates who have voted, or pledge to vote, for renters, city services and the police accountability office voters created, and whose donors don’t overlap with Republican campaigns. Small-donor numbers come from the city’s Small Donor Elections data. Donor numbers come from Oregon’s campaign finance records; Receipts shows exactly how we count. Records and endorsements are linked to their sources below. We list what critics say too, so nothing here surprises you.
